Key Learnings — Day 6: Resume Optimization with AI (ATS)

1. ATS optimization is about content, not visual redesign
The biggest surprise — my "before" and "after" resumes look almost identical visually. That's because ATS-friendly resumes are supposed to stay plain, single-column, and free of tables/graphics. The real optimization happened in the wording and structure, not the design.

2. Word choice directly affects ATS scoring
Swapping passive verbs like "Built" and "Designed" for stronger action verbs like "Engineered," "Implemented," and "Deployed" isn't just stylistic — ATS parsers score resumes partly on action-verb strength and keyword density.

3. A Professional Summary matters more than I expected
My original resume jumped straight into Education with no framing. Adding a 3-line summary at the top gave both ATS systems and human recruiters immediate context on my target role (AI/ML) before they even reach my projects.

4. Standard section headers aren't optional
Non-standard or creatively-named headers can fail to parse correctly in ATS systems. Sticking to conventional labels (Professional Summary, Education, Projects, Skills, Certifications) ensures the resume is actually machine-readable.

5. Honesty constraints make AI tools more useful, not less
The prompt explicitly instructed Claude to never invent achievements, skills, or experience — only reword and restructure existing truthful content. This is a good reminder that AI resume tools should enhance real information, not fabricate it.

6. Context and prompt structure produce more reliable output
Just like Day 5's context engineering lesson, giving Claude a highly structured prompt (with explicit output format: ATS Score + Final Resume) produced a clean, consistent, directly usable result — instead of a vague back-and-forth.

7. Having no "Experience" section is fine — Projects can carry that weight
As a student with no formal work experience, I learned that a strong Projects section (with real, well-described technical work like Circuit.IQ) can substitute effectively, rather than padding a fake Experience section.
