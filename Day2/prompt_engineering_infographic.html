Field guide — 01

Prompt engineering

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing clear, structured instructions so an AI model produces the response you actually want — on the first try, not the fifth. It's less about magic words and more about giving the model the same clarity you'd give a capable new colleague: who they're acting as, what exactly you need, and what "done" looks like.

Section 02

Five core techniques

◒
Role & context
Tell the model who to act as and what background it should assume before it starts.
▭
Specificity & scope
Say exactly what's included, what's excluded, and how big or narrow the task is.
↝
Process guidance
Spell out the steps or method the model should follow, not just the end goal.
▤
Output formatting
Specify the shape of the answer — list, table, length, headers — so it's usable immediately.
∴
Chain-of-thought
Ask the model to reason through the problem step by step before giving a final answer.

Pitfall — ambiguity
Vague wording lets the model guess your meaning, and it often guesses wrong.
Fix: add specificity — name the topic, audience, and goal directly.
Pitfall — scope creep
Open-ended asks invite the model to cover everything, producing bloated answers.
Fix: set boundaries — state what to leave out and how deep to go.
Pitfall — missing constraints
No format or length guidance means you get whatever shape the model defaults to.
Fix: define output rules — format, length, tone, structure.
Section 04

Vague to precise, one layer at a time

01
Start — vague prompt
"Write about dogs."
02
+ Role & context
"You are an experienced veterinarian writing about dogs."
03
+ Audience
"...writing for first-time puppy owners."
04
+ Specificity & scope
"...explain the 3 most important things to know about caring for a puppy in its first year."
05
+ Process guidance
"First list the three needs, then explain each in 2–3 sentences."
06
+ Output formatting
"Format as a bulleted list with bold headers, under 250 words."
07
+ Chain-of-thought
"Before answering, briefly think through what a first-time owner would find most confusing."
08
Final — precise prompt
"You are an experienced veterinarian writing for first-time puppy owners. Before answering, briefly think through what a new owner would find most confusing. Then explain the 3 most important things to know about caring for a puppy in its first year — first list the three needs, then explain each in 2–3 sentences. Format as a bulleted list with bold headers, under 250 words."
Section 05

Before / after, in practice

Email drafting
Before
"Write an email to my team about the deadline."
After
"You are a project manager. Write a short, direct email to my 5-person design team letting them know the Friday deadline is now Wednesday. Explain the reason in one sentence, keep it under 100 words, and end with a clear next step."
Data summary
Before
"Summarize this sales data."
After
"Act as a sales analyst. Summarize this quarter's data for a non-technical executive. Identify the top 3 trends, think through what's driving each one, then present findings as a 3-bullet list with one supporting number per bullet."
Creative brainstorm
Before
"Give me some birthday party ideas."
After
"You are an event planner on a budget. Suggest 5 birthday party themes for a 7-year-old, indoors, under $150. For each, give a one-line concept and one easy activity. Present as a numbered list."
Section 06 — for the designer

Layout specs for a shareable infographic

Dimensions
Social carousel: 1080×1350px (4:5) per panel.
Single-image share: 1080×1920px (story) or 1200×1600px (print-friendly A4-ish, 150dpi).
Color palette
Paper #FAF8F3 · blue #2C4A6E · amber #D98C2B · teal #3E8E82 · red #B24B33 · ink #1C2430
Typography
Headers: Space Grotesk, semibold–bold.
Body: Inter, regular–medium, 1.5–1.7 line height.
Prompt text / labels: JetBrains Mono, for a "technical instruction" feel.
Iconography
Simple line-weight glyphs (outline style, 1.5–2px stroke) over illustration — clock, list, gear, chat-bubble, arrow. Use a blueprint grid background and corner brackets to reinforce the "technical drafting" feel. Keep icons monochrome; let the palette carry meaning, not the icons.
Takeaway
A good prompt isn't longer — it's more specific. Tell the model who to be, what counts as done, and how to show its work.
