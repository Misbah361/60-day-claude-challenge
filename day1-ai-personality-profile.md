# AI Personality Profile — Day 1

## 1. AI working style
Treats AI like a research partner, not a vending machine. Goes deep rather than wide on any single thread — asking follow-ups that peel back assumptions, cross-checking claims, and using AI across the full stack of work: coding, research, and writing, rather than sticking to one lane. Comfortable switching modes mid-session as the problem demands it.

## 2. Strengths & weaknesses
**Strengths:** First-principles thinking, structured reasoning, intellectual patience, comfort with ambiguity while still wanting rigor underneath it.

**Weaknesses:** Structured thinkers can over-index on getting the "complete" answer before acting — risk of analysis paralysis or endlessly refining a prompt instead of shipping the output. Also worth watching: because of a mix-of-everything usage style, depth in any one AI skill (e.g. prompt engineering, agentic workflows, fine-tuning) may lag breadth.

## 3. Type of AI user
The **Systems Researcher** — someone who uses AI as a thinking instrument as much as a productivity tool. Less "give me the answer," more "help me build the right mental model."

## 4. Best future career paths
- AI research (applied ML, alignment, evals)
- Technical/AI product research or strategy
- AI-augmented academic research (any field, using AI as a research multiplier)
- Founding technical products where the moat is a well-reasoned system, not just a fast build

## 5. Learning & decision-making style
Bottom-up and structural — wants to understand *why* something works before trusting it, and builds decisions on verified foundations rather than pattern-matching from vibes. Slower to a first answer, but the answers hold up better under pressure.

## 6. What makes this different from most AI users
Most AI users optimize for speed of output. This approach optimizes for correctness of understanding, using AI as a Socratic partner — rarer than it sounds, since most usage patterns show shallow, transactional prompting.

## 7. What to improve to go elite
- Build a bias toward shipping/testing hypotheses faster, not just reasoning them out
- Develop actual technical fluency with how the models work (context windows, tool-use, agents) rather than only using them as black-box oracles
- Practice compressing structured thinking into tighter prompts — depth doesn't require length

## 8. Fun AI title
**The First-Principles Whisperer**

## 9. Cinematic character description
Dim room, three monitors humming, one showing a half-finished proof, one showing a terminal, one showing a half-written argument. No touching the keyboard until the whole problem is mapped out mentally — then movement is fast, decisive, precise. Not a hacker. An architect who happens to code.
